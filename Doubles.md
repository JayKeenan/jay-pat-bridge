# Doubles — which word is this

**Not law.** Working picture: **Gavin.** We have not locked ceilings.

Takeout of *their* opening: `COMPETITIVE.md` §2. This page is the family, major-first.

Matchpoints. Stay safe. Plus scores.

---

## The one idea

A double is a **word**. The auction tells you which word.

**Takeout** (they opened, we have not bid) is the wide word: *all* the unbid suits. Partner may pick a minor.

**Negative** and **responsive** are **not** that word. They are **major-oriented.** The other suits, on these doubles, means **the other major**. Minors are a leftover when no unbid major remains — not the point.

| Job | Means |
|-----|--------|
| **Takeout** | “I can play the *unbid* suits. You pick.” All of them. |
| **Negative / responsive** | “I have the **other major**.” |
| **Support double** | “I have **three** of *your* major.” |
| **Penalty** | “I have *their* suit. I want to defend.” |

If you remember one leak: **responsive is not a stack in their suit.** That double is the other major. A heart holding after they raised hearts **passes**.

## A map (read top to bottom)

First match wins. Usual Gavin ceilings in parentheses — **not** our lock until we sit them in.

| Auction | The call is |
|---------|-------------|
| They **opened a suit**. We have not bid a suit. | **Takeout** (all unbid suits) |
| **We** opened. They **overcalled**. Partner has **not** shown a suit. | **Negative** — the **other major** |
| Partner **doubled or overcalled**. They **raised**. | **Responsive** — the **other major** |
| **We** opened. Partner responded **1♥ or 1♠**. They bid. | **Support double** — **three** of partner’s major |
| We have a **major fit** and they bid a suit; we **cue-bid** it | **Support cue-bid** — **limit+ raise** |
| We have **raised**, bid **NT**, or already said penalty | **Penalty** |
| They opened **1NT** | Later |

**Redouble** of their takeout double of our 1M is “I have values” (10+), not a raise. Later (that sitting is transfers).

## Takeout — they opened

> Short in their suit. Can stand **all** the unbid suits, especially the unbid major(s). Opening values with shape — or a strong hand that will bid again.

Partner is supposed to **bid**. Sitting for penalty is rare: a real stack, and you **pass**. That pass *converts* takeout into penalty.

This is the only double that is allowed to be “any suit you pick,” including a minor.

**Double vs overcall** (`COMPETITIVE.md`):

```
Real 5+ suit (overcall quality)  →  overcall
   (unless too strong → double, then bid the suit)

No overcall suit, short theirs, places to play  →  double

Four-card majors  →  double (we do not overcall four)
```

### Example — takeout, both majors

**Dealer East · neither vul**

East opens **1♦**. South `♠ AJxx  ♥ KQxx  ♦ x  ♣ Jxxx`.

**Double.** Two four-card majors, short diamonds. 1♥ or 1♠ would be a four-card overcall — off.

If South had `♠ AQ10xx  ♥ Kxx  ♦ x  ♣ Jxx`, that is **1♠**. Real suit, lead, strain.

**Tell the opponents:** Nothing.

## Negative — we opened, they overcalled

You are **responder**. They bid a suit. You have not named a suit yet.

**The other major** (usually four), and enough to compete. Not “both unbid suits.” Not their suit.

| Auction | Double shows |
|---------|----------------|
| 1♦ (1♠) X | **Four hearts** |
| 1♣ (1♥) X | **Exactly four spades.** 1♠ is **five**. |
| 1♣ (1♦) X | **Both majors** (the two unbid majors) |
| 1♥ (1♠) X | No unbid major left. Leftover: **minors.** This is the exception, not the method. |

| Instead of X | When |
|--------------|------|
| **Bid the major** | You have **five** and can name it. Negative is four. |
| **Pass** | Too weak, **or** a **stack** in their suit (hope opener reopens). |
| **1NT** | Natural, stopper, no other-major to show. |

Values (usual card): enough to stand the next level. Roughly **6+** at one; more as they climb. Do not negative-double a three-level overcall with a yarborough.

**The cost:** you cannot penalty-double their 1♠ with KQJ10x. You **pass**. If opener is short, he reopens with double; you pass again — penalty. If opener has length, they play 1♠. We buy the four-card major.

Our **1♣ is a suit**. Negative after 1♣ is the **same voice** as after 1♦.

### Example — four hearts, not five

**Dealer South · neither vul**

South **1♦**. West **1♠**.  
North `♠ xx  ♥ KQxx  ♦ xxx  ♣ Axxx` — 10, four hearts.

**Double.** Not 2♥ — that is five.

If North had `♠ xx  ♥ KQxxx  ♦ xx  ♣ Axxx`, **2♥**. Five. Stay out of the gadget.

### Example — exactly four spades

South **1♣**. West **1♥**.  
North `♠ KQxx  ♥ xx  ♦ Axx  ♣ xxxx`.

**Double** — four spades. **1♠** would be five (same idea as peace: 1♠ then a force is five; here the overcall stole the one-level, so X is the four-card spade).

### Example — stack: pass

1♦ – (1♠) – ?  
North `♠ KQJ10x  ♥ xx  ♦ xx  ♣ xxxx`.

**Pass.** Double would show hearts. If South reopens with double, North **passes**.

## Responsive — partner entered, they raised

Partner **takeout-doubled or overcalled**. They **raised**. Your double.

**The other major.** Not a stack. Not “both minors” as the message.

| Auction | X means |
|---------|---------|
| `(1♥) – X – (2♥) – X` | **Spades** (the other major) |
| `(1♦) – 1♥ – (2♦) – X` | **Spades** |
| `(1♥) – 1♠ – (2♥) – X` | No unbid major. Leftover: **minors** (and you do not have a spade raise) |
| `(1♥) – X – (2♥) – 2♠` | You *named* spades instead |

**Raise partner** if you have a raise. Responsive is when you **don’t**, and you still have the other major to look for.

**Not** responsive if they bid a **new** suit. That auction is later. Do not invent a third double there.

### Example — the leak

**Dealer East · neither vul**

East **1♥**, South **double**, West **2♥**.  
North `♠ xx  ♥ KQJ10  ♦ xx  ♣ xxxx`.

**Pass.** Hearts are *their* suit. Double would show **spades**.

North `♠ Axxx  ♥ x  ♦ Kxxx  ♣ Jxxx`: **double**. Four spades. That is the whole point. Diamonds and clubs are along for the ride.

## Support double — opener has three

**We** opened. Partner responded **1♥ or 1♠**. They bid.

| Opener | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Double** | **Exactly three** of partner’s major |
| **Raise** | **Four** (or more) |
| **XX** (they doubled 1M) | Three — support *redouble*, same idea |
| Pass / NT / a suit | Not three, or too high for the gadget |

Usual ceiling: **through 2♠**.

This is not takeout of *their* suit. Responder already showed the major. Opener is talking about the **fit**.

It earns its keep after **1♣ / 1♦ – 1M**. Uncontested, a raise is four and already **15–17 or shapely** (12–14 with four of a major opened 1NT). When they interfere, you still need “three.” That is the support double.

### Example — three, not four

**Dealer South · neither vul**

South `♠ AJx  ♥ Kxx  ♦ AQxx  ♣ xxx` — 1♦ (too good for 1NT).  
North **1♠**. West **2♣**.

**Double** = three spades. **2♠** = four.

## Support cue-bid — the good raise

Same sitting, the other half. **Not a double.** It belongs here because mixed **3M is already off in competition** (`SYSTEM.md` / [Majors](Majors.md)): the jump raise is **preemptive**. The limit raise lost its uncontested home (`3♦`). The cue-bid is that home.

They bid a suit. We have a **major** to raise.

| Call | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Simple raise** | Compete. Fit, not an invite. |
| **Jump raise** | Preemptive (mixed is off) |
| **Cue-bid** of their suit | **Limit raise or better.** The good raise. |

Same idea whether **we** opened the major or **partner overcalled** it. Cue of their suit = “we own this major, I have a real raise.”

A courtesy 8-count **raises**. An 11–12 with four **cues**. Do not cue with the 8 — that is how a competitive 2♠ becomes a game you never had.

### Example — they overcall, we have a limit raise

**Dealer South · neither vul**

South **1♠**. West **2♥**.  
North `♠ Qxxx  ♥ xx  ♦ AKxx  ♣ xxx` — four spades, limit.

**3♥** (cue). Not 3♠ — that is the preempt. Not 3♦ — that was the uncontested limit, and 3♦ now would be a suit.

If North had `♠ Qxx  ♥ xx  ♦ Kxxx  ♣ xxxx` — 8, three spades: **2♠**. Compete. Stay safe.

**Tell the opponents:** Cue-bid as a raise is conventional. Explain if they ask: “limit raise or better in spades.”

## Penalty — we want to defend

The map says penalty when takeout and the other-major doubles have already had their say: we raised, we bid NT, they are high, they ran.

**Converting:** partner made a *takeout* double. You have a stack. **Pass.** You turned his takeout into penalty.

Matchpoints: +200 vs their 2M is a great score. −590 because you doubled them into game is a zero.

Penalty of a **one-level overcall** is what we sold to play negative. Do not grab it back.

## How to apply, practically

Before you double:

1. **Who has spoken for our side?**
2. **Is there an unbid major?**
3. **Did they *raise*, or bid a *new* suit?**

| You are… | They did… | Default |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| Unbid, they opened | Suit | **Takeout** (all unbid) — or overcall / pass |
| Responder, we opened | Overcall | **Negative** = other major, or bid five, or pass |
| Advancer, partner entered | **Raise** | **Responsive** = other major, or raise partner, or pass |
| Opener, partner bid 1M | Overcall | **Support X** = three; raise = four |
| Anyone with a major fit | Their suit | **Cue** = limit+; raise = compete; jump = preempt |
| We already have a fit or NT | Anything | Double is **penalty** |

**Stuck?** Low-level doubles are almost never “I have their suit.” Negative and responsive are almost never “bid a minor, partner.” High-level doubles more often are penalty.

**Stay safe.** Negative and responsive **compete**. They are not invitations unless the later auction says so. The cue-bid *is* the invite (or better). Do not mix those two.

## What this page is not

- Not a lock of **through 3♠** (negative / responsive) or **through 2♠** (support X). Usual Gavin ceilings; we sit them in next.
- Not vs their **1NT**, not lead-directing, not they-doubled-our-1M (transfers).
- Not Jordan 2NT, not mixed jump-cues. Cue = limit+. Jump raise = preempt. That is the whole raise menu in competition.
