# Doubles — which word is this

**Not law.** Ideas only. We have not locked ceilings, support doubles, or war.

Takeout doubles of *their* opening already have a working note in `COMPETITIVE.md` §2. This page is the family: what a double is *for*, so the next sitting can lock one card instead of four gadgets.

Matchpoints. Stay safe. Plus scores.

---

## The one idea

A double is a **word**. The auction tells you which word. There are two jobs, and then three common names for the first job:

| Job | Means | Classic name |
|-----|--------|----------------|
| **Takeout** | “I can play the *other* suits. You pick.” | Takeout, **negative**, **responsive** |
| **Penalty** | “I have *their* suit. I want to defend.” | Penalty (and converting takeout by passing) |
| **Support** | “I have **three** of *your* major.” | Support double — a different job |

Negative and responsive are **not** new ideas. They are takeout, said by a different player, after a different auction. Support is the odd one: it is about *our* fit, not their suit.

If you remember one leak: **responsive is not a stack in their suit.** That double is “the other suits,” same as takeout. Penalty of their raise is a **pass** (or a later penalty double once the map says penalty).

## A map (read top to bottom)

First match wins. This is the usual modern card — **not** our lock.

| Auction | The double is |
|---------|----------------|
| They **opened a suit**. We have not bid a suit. | **Takeout** |
| **We** opened. They **overcalled**. Partner has **not** shown a suit yet. | **Negative** (takeout — the unbid major(s)) |
| Partner **doubled or overcalled**. They **raised**. | **Responsive** (takeout — the unbid suits) |
| **We** opened. Partner responded **1♥ or 1♠**. They bid. | **Support** (exactly **three** of partner’s major), if we play it |
| We have **raised**, bid **NT**, or already said penalty | **Penalty** |
| High enough that takeout is no longer the story | **Penalty** (or a save — later) |
| They opened **1NT** | Different page. Later. |

**Redouble** of their takeout double of our 1M is “I have values,” not a raise. Later.

## Takeout — they opened

> Short in their suit. Can stand the unbid suits, especially the unbid major(s). Opening values with shape — or a strong hand that will bid again.

Partner is supposed to **bid**. Sitting for penalty is rare: you need a real stack in their suit, and you **pass**. That pass *converts* takeout into penalty.

**Double vs overcall** (already in `COMPETITIVE.md`):

```
Real 5+ suit (overcall quality)  →  overcall
   (unless too strong → double, then bid the suit)

No overcall suit, short theirs, places to play  →  double

Four-card majors  →  double (we do not overcall four)
```

### Example — takeout, not a suit

**Dealer East · neither vul**

East opens **1♦**. South `♠ AJxx  ♥ KQxx  ♦ x  ♣ Jxxx`.

**Double.** Two four-card majors, short diamonds. 1♥ or 1♠ would be a four-card overcall — off.

If South had `♠ AQ10xx  ♥ Kxx  ♦ x  ♣ Jxx`, that is **1♠**. Real suit, lead, strain.

**Tell the opponents:** Nothing (takeout of a one-level suit opening is expected).

## Negative — we opened, they overcalled

You are **responder**. They bid a suit. You have not named a suit yet.

The double says: **I have the unbid major(s)** (usually four), and enough to compete. It does **not** say “I have their suit.”

| Instead of X | When |
|--------------|------|
| **Bid the major** | You have **five** (you can name it cheaply). Negative is the *four*-card major. |
| **Pass** | Too weak to compete, **or** a **stack** in their suit (you want partner to reopen with double so you can sit). |
| **1NT / 2NT** | Natural, stopper, no four-card unbid major you needed to show. |

Values, starting points (usual card, not lock): enough to respond at that level. Roughly **6+** at the one-level; more as they climb. Do not negative-double a three-level overcall with a yarborough.

**The cost:** you cannot penalty-double their 1♠ overcall with KQJ10x of spades. You **pass**. If opener is short, he reopens with double (takeout). You pass again — now it is penalty. If opener has length in spades, he cannot reopen, and they play 1♠. That is the trade. We buy the four-card major; we give up the one-level penalty double.

Our **1♣ is a suit**, not a strong club. Negative after 1♣ is the **same voice** as after 1♦.

### Example — four hearts, not five

**Dealer South · neither vul**

South opens **1♦**. West overcalls **1♠**.  
North `♠ xx  ♥ KQxx  ♦ xxx  ♣ Axxx` — 10, four hearts.

**Double** (negative). Not 2♥ — that is five hearts (or a hand that wants to name hearts as a suit).

If North had `♠ xx  ♥ KQxxx  ♦ xx  ♣ Axxx`, **2♥**. Five. Stay out of the gadget.

**Tell the opponents:** Negative doubles go on the card. When we lock a ceiling, that is the explanation if they ask.

### Example — stack: pass, do not “penalty double”

Same auction: 1♦ – (1♠) – ?  
North `♠ KQJ10x  ♥ xx  ♦ xx  ♣ xxxx`.

**Pass.** If we are playing negative, double would show hearts, not spades. If South reopens with double, North **passes** — that is the penalty conversion.

## Responsive — partner entered, they raised

Partner **takeout-doubled or overcalled**. They **raised** their suit. Now it is your double.

> I can play the **unbid** suits. I do **not** have a stack in theirs.

That is takeout, one seat later. Same leak as negative, louder, because their raise *looks* like a penalty situation.

| Auction | X means |
|---------|---------|
| `(1♥) – X – (2♥) – X` | Takeout of hearts: minors, or spades + a minor |
| `(1♥) – 1♠ – (2♥) – X` | Takeout: the minors (you do not have a spade raise, you do not have hearts) |
| `(1♥) – X – (2♥) – 2♠ / 3♣ / 3♦` | You picked a suit instead |

**Raise partner** if you have a raise. Responsive is what you do when you **don’t**.

**Not** responsive if they bid a **new** suit. That is a different auction (often penalty of the last suit, or a pass). Do not invent a third double there until we lock one.

### Example — the leak

**Dealer East · neither vul**

East **1♥**, South **double**, West **2♥**.  
North `♠ xx  ♥ KQJ10  ♦ xx  ♣ xxxx`.

**Pass.** This is **not** a responsive double. Hearts are *their* suit. You have a stack — you want to defend only if South’s double gets converted, and here West raised; sitting in 2♥ doubled is a different (penalty) story we have not locked. A responsive double would show **spades and minors**, which you do not have.

North `♠ Axxx  ♥ x  ♦ Kxxx  ♣ Jxxx` after the same start: **double** (responsive). Places to play. Not hearts.

## Support — we opened, they bid over 1M

The odd one. Not takeout of *their* suit.

**We** opened. Partner responded **1♥ or 1♠**. They overcall (or bid). If we play this:

| Opener | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Double** | **Exactly three** of partner’s major |
| **Raise** | **Four** (or more) |
| Pass / NT / a suit | Not three-card support (or too high for the gadget) |

Usual ceiling: **through 2♠**. Above that, raise with four and live with three another way.

**We have not locked this.** It is on almost every modern card (including Gavin). The point of the paper is that you not confuse it with negative: *responder* already showed the major. This double is **opener** talking about the fit.

It earns its keep after **1♣ / 1♦ – 1M**. Uncontested, a raise of their major is four cards and already **15–17 or shapely** (the 12–14 with four of a major opened 1NT). When they interfere, you still need a way to say “three.” That is the support double.

### Example — three, not four (if we play it)

**Dealer South · neither vul**

South `♠ AJx  ♥ Kxx  ♦ AQxx  ♣ xxx` — opened 1♦ (too good for 1NT).  
North **1♠**. West **2♣**.

If support doubles are on: **double** = three spades. **2♠** = four.  
If they are off: you have no cheap “three” — you pass or bid 2NT / 3♣ and hope.

## Penalty — we want to defend

The map says penalty when takeout has already had its say, or we have already described: we raised, we bid NT, they are high, they ran.

**Converting:** partner made a takeout double. You have a stack in their suit. **Pass.** You did not “make a penalty double.” You turned *his* takeout into one.

Matchpoints: +200 vs their 2M is a great score. −590 because you doubled them into game is a zero. **Do not double a contract they were not going to bid** unless you can beat it on a bad day too.

Penalty of a **one-level overcall** is the thing we sold to play negative. Do not grab it back at the table.

## How to apply, practically

Before you double, two questions:

1. **Who has already spoken for our side?**
2. **Did they *raise*, or bid a *new* suit?**

Then:

| You are… | They did… | Default |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| Unbid, they opened | Suit opening | Takeout (or overcall / pass) |
| Responder, we opened | Overcall | Negative, or bid your five-card major, or pass |
| Advancer, partner entered | **Raise** | Responsive, or raise partner, or pass |
| Opener, partner bid 1M | Overcall | Support *if on*; else raise = four, pass with three |
| Anyone, we already have a fit or NT | Anything | Penalty if you double |

**Stuck?** Low-level doubles are almost never “I have their suit.” High-level doubles more often are. Do not invent a new meaning because the hand is ugly.

**Stay safe.** A negative or responsive double is a **compete** call. It is not an invitation unless the later auction says so. A courtesy 6 that cannot stand the next level **passes**.
