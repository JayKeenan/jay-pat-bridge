# 6. <span style="color:#1565C0">1♥ / 1♠ – 1NT</span> — extras on the next round

**Law:** SYSTEM.md §6 (SF 1NT) and §7  
**Status:** Stub  
**Tell the opponents:** **Announce** 1NT — “semi-forcing.”

<span style="color:#1565C0;font-weight:700">1NT</span> is semi-forcing. Opener passes only with a hand that would **not** accept a limit raise.

**15 to a bad 18 5332 does not bid 2NT.** 2NT is **18–20**. That hand rebids **2♣ or 2♦**, often a **three-card** minor, and catches up later.

**Hearts only:** `1♥ – 1♠ – 1NT` = 15 to a bad 18, 5/6 hearts. That is the cheap name for the strong notrump. After 1♠ there is no such step.

## Example — three-card minor, extras later

**Dealer South · neither vul**

| | ♠ | ♥ | ♦ | ♣ |
|--|--|--|--|--|
| **S** | AQx | AJxxx | Kxx | Kx |
| **N** | xxx | xx | Qxxx | Axxx |

| West | North | East | South |
|------|-------|------|-------|
| | | | 1♥ |
| Pass | <span style="color:#1565C0;font-weight:700">1NT</span> | Pass | 2♦ |
| Pass | Pass | Pass | |

**Why:** South is 16, 5332, too good to pass 1NT and too small for 2NT (18–20). 2♦ is three diamonds. North is happy to play 2♦.

**Tell the opponents:** Announce 1NT “semi-forcing.” 2♦ is natural (may be three). If they ask later why South bid again with a 16-count, that is the method — extras come next if responder moves.
